# Deception and surprise: a feint works only when real strength stands behind it

Updated: 7 November 2026. Project: strategy course. Tag: `[Strategy-Course-Deception]`.

Notes merged here: `sources/AOW/notes/u03.md` (index: `sources/AOW/INDEX.md`; unit u03, chapters V to VIII). **Single source**: this sheet does not cross several books. It crosses the voices inside one book: Sun Tzu's verses, the classical commentators quoted by Giles, and Giles's own remarks (1910). Divergences are therefore shown between these voices.

Convention: "according to AOW u03 §N" = what the source says, with the voice named; "proposal" = a reading or a link made by Claude, absent from the source.

## In one sentence

According to AOW u03, deception means controlling what the enemy sees so that he divides, moves or attacks where you want, and it only works when the hidden reality (discipline, courage, strength) is the opposite of the show (AOW u03 §26, §34, §74).

## A feint needs the real opposite quality behind it (importance 5/5)

- According to AOW u03 §26 (Sun Tzu), feigned disorder needs discipline, feigned fear needs courage, feigned weakness needs strength.
- According to AOW u03 §28 to §32 (Sun Tzu), each feint has its base: subdivision under apparent disorder, stored energy under apparent timidity, dispositions under apparent weakness.
- According to AOW u03 §24 and §25 (Sun Tzu, Mei Yao-ch'en), pre-set subdivisions and signals make the scattering of battle look like disorder without being disorder.
- According to AOW u03 §27 (Tu Mu), the purpose of each feint is to lure or trap the enemy.

See also: "Direct and indirect" below; sheet `[Strategy-Course-Leadership]` (not written yet) for subdivision and signals.

## Surprise is defined by what the enemy expects (importance 5/5)

- According to AOW u03 §8 (Sun Tzu), the direct method engages and the indirect method wins.
- According to AOW u03 §12 to §16 (Sun Tzu), only two methods exist, but their combinations are endless and each leads into the other like a circle.
- According to AOW u03 §6 (Giles), cheng is any move the enemy is watching, ch'i the one that surprises him; a surprise that is spotted becomes cheng.
- According to AOW u03 §6 (Ho Shih, Han Hsin at Lin-chin), the open march was cheng and the river crossing in wooden tubs was ch'i. Anecdote, **to verify**.
- According to AOW u03 §9 (Chang Yu), indirect means hitting the flanks or the rear.

## Hidden dispositions force the enemy to divide (importance 4/5)

- According to AOW u03 §74 to §83 (Sun Tzu), if we see him and he cannot see us, we stay one body and he must split to guard every point; reinforcing everywhere makes him weak everywhere.
- According to AOW u03 §98 and §100 (Sun Tzu), the highest form of disposition is to conceal it, so that no spy or clever officer can plan against it.
- According to AOW u03 §105 (Sun Tzu), a winning method is not repeated.
- According to AOW u03 §82 and §84 (Giles quoting Frederick the Great and Henderson), the same idea in European military writing: protect the main object, and make the enemy disperse before concentrating on each part.

See also: sheet `[Strategy-Course-Planning]` (not written yet) for concentration and weak points.

## Appearances and baits move the enemy (importance 4/5)

- According to AOW u03 §34, §36 and §37 (Sun Tzu), a skilled general keeps the enemy moving with false appearances, gives up something for him to grab, and waits with picked troops.
- According to AOW u03 §35 (Tu Mu), show weakness if stronger to lure him, show strength if weaker to keep him away.
- According to AOW u03 §70 and §72 (Sun Tzu), attack a place he must relieve to force battle; put something strange in his way to avoid it.
- According to AOW u03 §73 (Tu Mu, Chu-ko Liang's open gates; Giles calls it "bluff"), an empty-looking city made the attacker fear an ambush. Anecdote, **to verify**.
- According to AOW u03 §35 (Tu Mu, Sun Pin's shrinking camp fires: 100,000, then 50,000, then 20,000, in 341 B.C.), a fake desertion drew the pursuer into an ambush. Anecdote, **to verify**; Giles prefers the Shih Chi's less dramatic ending.
- According to AOW u03 §129 (Tu Mu, Chao She, 270 B.C.: stopped after 30 li, fortified 28 days, then marched 2 days and 1 night), a fake delay reported by spies hid a fast march. Anecdote, **to verify**.
- According to AOW u03 §128 and §169 (Sun Tzu), the "artifice of deviation": take the long road, lure the enemy aside, arrive first.

## Speed turns a feint into a surprise (importance 3/5)

- According to AOW u03 §149 and §151 (Sun Tzu), practise dissimulation and move only for a real gain.
- According to AOW u03 §153 to §161 (Sun Tzu), be fast as wind, still as a mountain, keep plans dark as night and strike like a thunderbolt; Mei Yao-ch'en adds that wind leaves no tracks (AOW u03 §154).
- According to AOW u03 §19 and §22 (Sun Tzu, Giles), energy is the bent crossbow and decision the trigger; Giles reads decision as holding back until the effective range.
- According to AOW u03 §150 (Giles), Turenne stressed deceiving the enemy about one's numbers.

## Guarding against the enemy's deception (importance 3/5)

- According to AOW u03 §197 and §198 (Sun Tzu), do not pursue a feigned flight and do not swallow a bait.
- According to AOW u03 §33 (Chang Yu, Kao Tsu at Po-teng), the Hsiung-nu showed only weak men and beasts and the emperor, ignoring a warning, was surrounded. Anecdote, **to verify**.
- According to AOW u03 §93 to §95 (Sun Tzu), provoke the enemy to learn his intentions and force him to show his weak spots; Chang Yu: read his joy or anger (AOW u03 §94).

## Attacking the enemy's mind (importance 2/5)

- According to AOW u03 §185 and §187 (Sun Tzu), an army can lose its spirit and its general his composure.
- According to AOW u03 §188 (Li Ching, quoted by Giles), attack includes the enemy's mental balance.
- According to AOW u03 §241 (Sun Tzu), hold out allurements so that the enemy rushes where you want.

## Points of agreement

- Sun Tzu, Tu Mu and Mei Yao-ch'en agree that a show of weakness or disorder rests on real order (AOW u03 §25 to §27).
- Sun Tzu and Tu Mu agree that the way to force a sheltered enemy out is to threaten something he must protect (AOW u03 §70, §71).
- Proposal: all voices treat deception as a means to divide or move the enemy, never as an end (AOW u03 §27, §35, §74 to §83).

## What is missing

- Only one unit of AOW is distilled: deception in chapters I (laying plans) and XIII (spies) is not covered yet (notes u02 and u05 pending in `sources/AOW/INDEX.md`).
- Every anecdote is to verify in the original histories (Shih Chi, Ch'ien Han Shu, Tso Chuan, San Kuo Chi), none of which is in the library (AOW u03 §6, §33, §35, §73, §129).
- Giles's text has corrupt passages and alternative readings (AOW u03 §11, §19, §133); a second translation would show which readings are solid.
- No modern source (military history, psychology of deception) is in the library to test these principles (see `catalog.md`).
